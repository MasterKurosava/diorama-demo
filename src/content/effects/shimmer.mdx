## Recipes

### A gold plan badge that glints

`style="gold"` with `trigger="loop"` sweeps a light over a short label every few seconds and draws nothing in between. A smaller `bevel` suits small text, where a wide bevel would round the letters off. Gold reads well on a light page; `colors` turns it into rose or white gold.

### Holographic foil for a limited drop

`style="holo"` turns the letters into a rainbow foil whose colors move with the viewing angle, with fine glitter that flashes as the angle changes. With `trigger="pointer"` a lamp follows the pointer over the letters. On a light page the foil is deeper and more saturated so the name stays readable. Point at the name below.

<Example id="effects/shimmer/drop" description="A sneaker drop announcement on a pale gray page, the shoe name in holographic foil lit by a lamp at the pointer." aspect="16 / 6" show={['html']} controls={[]} variants={[]} />

### Glass that sweeps once in view

`style="glass"` draws clear letters with a tinted rim, tinted by the text color or the first of `colors`. On a dark page the rim catches the light; on a light page it darkens like the edge of real glass, and the shadow carries the tint with a little gathered light inside it.

```html
<h2 data-dio="shimmer" data-dio-style="glass" data-dio-trigger="view" data-dio-colors="#4aa8ff">Clear thinking</h2>
```

## Notes

The bevel is built from the shape of each letter: a distance field of the text gives every pixel its distance to the nearest edge, and a rounded profile over `bevel` turns that into a slope, so the edges catch the studio's lights the way a real bevel would. Each line also curves back a little at the top and bottom, which is what puts the horizon of the studio across the middle of chrome letters. The outline and anti-aliasing of the letters are the browser's own: the shape is read from a raster of the text, not redrawn.

The reflections follow the pointer anywhere on the page and turn as the heading scrolls through the viewport. After the pointer stops they settle in a fraction of a second and nothing more is drawn. Set `follow` to 0 to hold them still, and `speed` to 0 to turn the sweep off.

The heading stays real text in the DOM: screen readers read it, search engines index it, and find in page works. A CSS filter hides the DOM text while the material is drawn, so give the text a color that reads on its own, since that is what shows without WebGL. Text on screen when the page opens is hidden until the material is ready, at most a few seconds.

Use a bold, large font: the bevel needs room, and thin strokes show little of the material. The canvas cannot draw `background-clip: text`, `-webkit-text-stroke` or vertical writing modes the way the browser does, and an element with any of them keeps its text as it is, with a warning in development. A `text-shadow` is fine: the effect casts its own shadow instead.

`style`, `trigger` and `colors` rebuild the effect when they change. `bevel` builds the surface again a moment after it changes; the other params change live.
