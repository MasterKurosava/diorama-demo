## Recipes

### A flip sign with two messages

`data-dio-reverse` prints a second text on the backs of the slats, so the closed panel reads as one message and the open one as the other. With `data-dio-trigger="hover"` and `angle="180"` the sign rests closed on its back and turns over where the pointer is; `back` paints the backs, and the second text takes whichever of the page and the text color stands out on it. On a phone a tap turns the whole sign over in a ripple from the finger, and the next tap turns it back.

The element's own text is the one screen readers read, search engines index and find in page finds: put the message that matters there and the alternative on the backs.

### A card display wave on the scroll

`style="tiles"` cuts the heading into square cards that flip over their diagonal in a ripple from the top left corner, like a stadium card display. With `data-dio-trigger="scroll"` the ripple follows the scroll both ways: it starts once `threshold` of the heading is in view and lies flat when the heading reaches the middle of the viewport. Scroll past this example to see it.

### A shop shutter for a launch

`style="vertical"` turns upright slats from left to right, like a shutter opening in front of a shop window. A bright `back` against a dark page reads as a painted shutter; `stagger` sets how fast the opening runs along the line.

```html
<h1 data-dio="blinds" data-dio-style="vertical" data-dio-back="#ffcf3a" data-dio-stagger="0.05">Opening night</h1>
```

## Notes

The slats are thin boards built in 3D and seen in perspective from in front of the heading, not a mask over flat text. Each one turns about its own axis and stands out of the page as it turns, so its edge catches the light and it casts a soft shadow on the slat next to it and on the page, longer the further it stands out. The lamp sits above the page on the side `light` names, and faces turned toward it brighten while faces turned away darken. The front of a slat is cut from the page's own background color, which fades in as it turns, so put the effect on a heading over a solid background; over an image or a gradient the fronts stay clear and only the letters turn.

When the wave is over, and between nudges of the pointer, the heading is real, selectable text again and the effect draws no frames. With the view trigger a heading the visitor has already seen stays as it is, and one on the first screen opens behind the page's intro. With the hover trigger the slats rest at `angle`, drawn, until the pointer comes near; `angle="0"` rests open. Under reduced motion the text is shown as it is and the engine does not load for it.

Leaving a page the slats close again and fade; entering one, they open as the page comes in. The element is hidden behind its copy while it is drawn, its own background too: put a background behind the heading on a parent. The canvas cannot draw `background-clip: text`, `text-shadow`, `-webkit-text-stroke` or vertical writing modes the way the browser does: an element with any of them keeps its text as it is, and a warning in development names the reason. `style`, `trigger`, `slats` and `reverse` rebuild the effect when they change; the other params change live.
