## Recipes

### A collection piece seen by flashlight

For a museum, an auction lot or a product with fine detail, keep part of the dim at rest with `rest`, so the picture reads as "in the dark" before the visitor reaches for it. A larger `radius` suits a large photo; `softness` near 1 gives a torch, near 0 a hard theater spot.

```html
<img
  src="/collection/astrolabe.avif"
  data-dio="spotlight"
  data-dio-radius="160"
  data-dio-rest="0.6"
/>
```

### An x-ray under the pointer

`style="reveal"` shows a second picture inside the circle: the movement behind a watch face, a building before and after renovation, a plan over a photo. Both pictures should share their framing, since the second one is drawn at the same `object-fit` and position as the first. With `dim="0"` the rest of the image stays as it is.

```html
<img
  src="/watch/front.avif"
  data-dio="spotlight"
  data-dio-style="reveal"
  data-dio-src="/watch/movement.avif"
  data-dio-dim="0"
  style="touch-action: none"
/>
```

### Find the detail

`style="blur"` blurs everything outside the light as well, which turns a busy interior or a map into a small game of looking.

```html
<img
  src="/rooms/loft.avif"
  data-dio="spotlight"
  data-dio-style="blur"
  data-dio-dim="0.3"
  data-dio-blur="16"
/>
```

## Notes

The light only draws while it fades or trails the pointer; a still pointer costs nothing. On touch screens it follows a pressed finger; add `touch-action: none` to the image when a drag should move the light instead of scrolling the page.

The picture stays a plain `<img>`, so its `alt` text is all a screen reader needs. Under reduced motion the light appears and moves without fades or smoothing.

`style` and `src` rebuild the effect; every other param changes live.
