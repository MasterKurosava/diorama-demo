## Recipes

### Color split only

Set `strength` to 0 and the image stays flat while its red and blue channels drift apart with the scroll. Stars, city lights and type on a dark ground show the fringes best, so it suits a music or night-event site where a full bend would be too much.

<Example id="effects/bend/split" description="A photo of the Milky Way over the Earth whose stars split into red and blue fringes while the page scrolls." show={['html']} />

### Bend without the color split

For product photos, where colored fringes look like a printing fault, keep the bend and turn the split off with 0.

```html
<img
  src="/img/chair-side.avif"
  data-dio="bend"
  data-dio-split="0"
/>
```

### A gentle bend for long articles

A reader of a long article scrolls fast through it. Halve both params and the figures still move with the page without pulling the eye away from the text.

```html
<img
  src="/img/harbor.avif"
  data-dio="bend"
  data-dio-strength="0.5"
  data-dio-split="0.5"
/>
```

### A showreel strip

`bend` takes a `<video>` as well. A muted loop of an agency's work, set between two sections, bows with the scroll like the photos around it and plays on at rest. A low `split` keeps the colors of the footage while the page moves.

```html
<video
  src="/reel/2026.webm"
  poster="/reel/2026.webp"
  muted
  loop
  autoplay
  playsinline
  data-dio="bend"
  data-dio-split="0.4"
></video>
```

## Notes

The bend follows the speed of the scroll, not its position, so an image at rest always matches its HTML. Scrolling from code bends it too, such as a smooth `scrollTo()` to an anchor or smooth scrolling with Lenis.

The image always bows up and down, the way a page scrolls. A carousel that slides with CSS transforms is not a scroll and leaves its images straight.
