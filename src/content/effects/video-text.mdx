## Recipes

### A photo inside the letters

An image URL in `src` puts a still picture in the letters instead of a film, and then nothing is drawn after the first frame. A product page can set the name of a color in the color itself. On a light page a little `dim` keeps the letters from washing out.

### The whole picture inside the text

`fit` frames the picture over the text as a block, like `object-fit` over an element. `cover` fills every letter and crops the picture to the text's proportions, which suits one wide line. `contain` keeps the whole picture visible, so letters past its edges stay empty: use it for a short, tall block of text.

```html
<h1
  class="poster"
  data-dio="video-text"
  data-dio-src="/media/stage.webm /media/stage.mp4"
  data-dio-fit="contain"
>
  Blue
  <br />
  Signal
</h1>
```

## Notes

The heading stays real text in the DOM: screen readers read it, search engines index it, and find in page works. While the effect draws, a CSS filter hides the DOM text without removing it. Give the text a color that reads on its own, since that is what shows before the first frame, without WebGL and under reduced motion off.

Use a large, heavy font: thin strokes show little of the picture. The film plays only while the text is on screen and the tab is visible, at most `fps` frames a second, and pauses otherwise. Under reduced motion it holds its first frame.

The canvas cannot draw some text the way the browser does: `background-clip: text`, `text-shadow`, `-webkit-text-stroke` and vertical writing modes. An element with any of them keeps its text as it is, and a warning in development names the reason. A background or border on the heading itself is hidden with it while the effect draws, so put them on a wrapper.

A video from another origin needs CORS headers: the effect loads it in CORS mode. `src` and `fit` rebuild the effect when they change.
