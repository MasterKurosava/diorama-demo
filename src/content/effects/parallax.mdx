## Recipes

### A depth map from the photo

`map` is a grayscale image of the same frame, white where things are near. The map of the ridges above comes from the photo itself, since haze makes far ridges lighter: inverted, blurred and saved at half the size, it weighs 70 KB. A depth estimation model gives one for any photo.

```html
<img src="/img/ridges.jpg" data-dio="parallax" data-dio-map="/img/ridges-depth.png" />
```

`focus` picks the depth that stays put. At 0.5 the middle ridges hold still while the near ones move one way and the far ones the other. At 0 the sky holds and everything in front of it moves.

### Without a map

Without `map` the whole picture moves as one layer: it drifts away from the pointer and travels down through its frame as the page scrolls. It needs nothing but the photo.

<Example id="effects/parallax/flat" description="A photo of a cliff above the sea that drifts inside its frame with the pointer and the scroll." show={['html']} controls={['depth', 'scroll', 'ease']} variants={[]} />

### A headline over a moving hero

The canvas draws the copy above the page, so text laid over the image needs a `z-index` above it. Keep `depth` low on a hero with text, so the picture moves under the words without pulling the eye from them.

<Example id="effects/parallax/hero" description="A hero image of a wave on sea steps that drifts behind a white headline." aspect="16 / 9" show={['html']} controls={[]} variants={[]} />

```css
.hero-copy {
  position: relative;
  z-index: 2;
}
```

### Scroll only

With `depth` at 0 the pointer does nothing and the picture only travels with the scroll. Use it where the pointer is busy with something else, such as a card that is also a link, or to make the motion the same on touch screens and desktops.

```html
<img
  src="/img/canyon.avif"
  data-dio="parallax"
  data-dio-depth="0"
  data-dio-scroll="0.2"
/>
```

## Notes

The picture is drawn slightly larger than its box so the moving edge never shows, which crops a little of it. Keep faces and text away from the edges of a photo you give it.

A depth map needs no special tool: any grayscale image of the same frame works, white near and black far. A map from a depth estimation model, a render pass of a 3D scene or a hand-painted mask all do. Where the depth jumps sharply, the far side shows a stretched sliver; blur the map by a few pixels to hide it.
