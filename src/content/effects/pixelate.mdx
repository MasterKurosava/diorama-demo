## Recipes

### Pieces that turn to pixels under the pointer

With `trigger="hover"` the image is sharp and breaks into blocks while the pointer is over it, which suits a retro or gaming portfolio and a gallery of digital art. A small `cell` and a short `duration` keep it playful rather than hiding the work.

### Cascade across a grid

Give each image a slightly longer `delay` than the one before it, so a row resolves one tile after another as it scrolls into view.

```html
<div class="grid">
  <img src="/work/one.avif" data-dio="pixelate" />
  <img
    src="/work/two.avif"
    data-dio="pixelate"
    data-dio-delay="0.15"
  />
  <img
    src="/work/three.avif"
    data-dio="pixelate"
    data-dio-delay="0.3"
  />
</div>
```

### Hexagons tied to the scroll

With `trigger="scroll"` the blocks shrink as the image rises to the middle of the viewport and grow again when the reader scrolls back. `style="hex"` with `motion="smooth"` gives a softer, honeycomb look for an art or science page.

```html
<img
  src="/plates/algae.avif"
  data-dio="pixelate"
  data-dio-trigger="scroll"
  data-dio-style="hex"
  data-dio-motion="smooth"
/>
```

## Notes

A reveal on the first screen plays as the page opens: the image is hidden until the effect draws its first frame, at most three seconds. An image the reader already saw as plain HTML stays sharp and never breaks up again. Under reduced motion the reveal is skipped, and hover fades the blocks in without growing them.

In `round` the page shows between the dots, so the look depends on the background behind the image.

List `pixelate` before color effects such as `tone` in `data-dio`, so they color the blocks as well.

`style`, `trigger` and `motion` rebuild the effect; `cell`, `duration` and `delay` change live.
