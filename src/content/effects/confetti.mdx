## Recipes

### Celebrate a sign-up when the server says yes

A click is too early for a form: the request can still fail. Use the `event` trigger and dispatch the event once your server answers, so the burst marks a sign-up that happened.

<Example id="effects/confetti/signup" description="A free trial form that bursts confetti once the sign-up request succeeds." aspect="16 / 6" show={['html']} controls={[]} variants={[]} />

```ts title="src/signup.ts"
const form = document.querySelector('form.signup') as HTMLFormElement

form.addEventListener('submit', async (event) => {
  event.preventDefault()
  const response = await fetch('/api/signup', {
    method: 'POST',
    body: new FormData(form),
  })
  if (response.ok) form.dispatchEvent(new Event('signup:done'))
})
```

### Rain on a donation thank-you page

The `view` trigger fires the first time half the section is in view, and `rain` lets the pieces fall from above the whole viewport for a few seconds. With `once` it rains one time on the page, however often the visitor scrolls back.

```html
<section
  class="thanks"
  data-dio="confetti"
  data-dio-trigger="view"
  data-dio-motion="rain"
  data-dio-count="300"
  data-dio-once="true"
>
  <h1>Thank you. Your gift plants 40 trees.</h1>
  <p>A receipt for your tax records is on its way to your inbox.</p>
</section>
```

### Fireworks when a course is complete

The `manual` trigger leaves the moment to your code: every increase of `fire` is one burst. Fire it when the last lesson is marked done, and let `fireworks` burst shells over the upper half of the viewport.

<Example id="effects/confetti/course" description="The last lesson of a course marked complete, with fireworks bursting over the page." aspect="16 / 9" show={['html']} controls={[]} variants={[]} />

```ts
import { app } from './diorama'

const course = document.querySelector('.cf-course')
const confetti = course && app.effects.get(course, 'confetti')
if (confetti) confetti.params.fire++
```

## Notes

Under reduced motion neither the module nor the engine loads for it, and the page stays as it is. Treat the burst as decoration: say what happened in the page itself, as the examples do in a status line.

The effect never cancels the click. A form still submits and a link still opens; when the page changes right away, the pieces fade out with it during a page transition.

The pieces are drawn in one instanced draw from a pool of 1200, and frames run only while pieces fly. The quality tier caps `count` at 80 on the low tier and 140 on the medium one, so markup that asks for 400 pieces gets 140 on a device at the medium tier.

`trigger`, `motion`, `origin`, `shapes`, `colors` and `seed` rebuild the effect when they change. Counts, speeds and angles change live and apply to the next burst.
