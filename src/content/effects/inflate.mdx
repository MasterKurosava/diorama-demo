## Recipes

### Button labels that puff up on hover

With `trigger="hover"` the label stays plain text until the pointer or keyboard focus reaches the button, then its letters inflate in a quick wave and go flat again when it leaves; on a phone a tap inflates the label and the next tap lets it go. Put the effect on the text inside the button, not on the button: the effect draws the element it sits on in its place, so the button's background, border and padding stay as they are. Shorter `duration` and `stagger` keep the wave quick.

### Matte clay for a craft studio

`style="soft"` turns the letters into matte clay: a broad soft light, no sharp highlight, and a darker seam where each letter meets the page. A lower `gloss` takes away the last of the sheen. It suits a wide, heavy font.

<Example id="effects/inflate/clay" description="A pottery class poster on a pale gray background with the name Mud Club in orange clay letters." aspect="16 / 7" show={['html']} controls={['gloss', 'depth']} variants={[]} />

### Gummy candy in its own color

`style="jelly"` makes the letters translucent: clear and bright at the rims, deeper in color where they are thick, and the page shows through them. `color` overrides the text color for the letters and their shadow.

```html
<h2 data-dio="inflate" data-dio-style="jelly" data-dio-color="#19b48a">Gummy bears</h2>
```

## Notes

The letters are built in 3D, not painted. A distance field of the glyphs is built once on the CPU, and the letters are inflated like a membrane under pressure, so straight strokes become round tubes, dots become domes and the joints of strokes bulge a little. Each letter is a mesh raised by that height and seen through the page camera, so it leans in perspective, and the normals of the surface take a studio light with a highlight and a soft shadow on the page. `depth` scales the height: 1 is round, larger is taller than wide.

The pointer dents the skin where it rests and deeper where it presses, the skin around the dent rises a little, and letters near it lean toward it. Everything springs back with a wobble when the pointer leaves or lets go. `squish` and `tilt` at 0 turn the dent and the lean off.

The text stays real text in the DOM: screen readers read it, search engines index it, and find in page works. While the letters are drawn the DOM text is hidden without being removed. With `trigger="hover"` the element is plain text at rest. Frames run only while letters inflate or go flat and while the dent and the lean move; at rest the effect draws nothing. Under reduced motion the letters are round from the start and do not move.

The effect looks best on bold or display fonts at 48 px and larger; hairlines of a thin serif inflate too, as fine glossy tubes. The canvas cannot draw `background-clip: text`, `text-shadow`, `-webkit-text-stroke` or vertical writing modes the way the browser does: an element with any of them keeps its text as it is, and a warning in development names the reason. `style`, `trigger` and `color` rebuild the effect when they change; the other params change live.
