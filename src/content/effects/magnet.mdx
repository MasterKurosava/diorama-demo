## Recipes

### Navigation links that turn to the pointer

`style="attract"` turns each letter near the pointer to face it, so a link seems to look at the visitor as the pointer approaches. The radius is measured in em of the text, so the default reach of 2.5 em covers a word or two of a 16 px link and does not wake the whole menu. A hover color in your CSS shows on the moving letters too. Hover the links below.

### A word mark that magnifies like a dock

`style="lift"` raises the letters under the pointer toward the viewer, so they grow in the page's perspective, and slides their neighbors along the line to make room. It suits a short, large word such as a studio name or a product name in a hero.

```html
<p class="mark" data-dio="magnet" data-dio-style="lift">studio</p>
```

### Loose letters for a party poster

`style="scatter"` tosses the letters up and apart, each at its own angle and spin. A low `damping` keeps them wobbling for a while after the pointer leaves, and a lower `stiffness` makes them float back more slowly.

```html
<h2 data-dio="magnet" data-dio-style="scatter" data-dio-damping="6" data-dio-stiffness="120">Party starts at nine</h2>
```

## Notes

Each letter is drawn on its own as a thin slab with beveled edges, lit from the upper left, so its face brightens or darkens as it turns, its sides show, and it casts a soft shadow shaped like the letter. A letter at rest is flat and square to the viewer in exactly your CSS color, so the hand-over to the real text is invisible. Shadows show best on a light background; under text smaller than about 40 px they fade out, so a small link stays crisp.

The element keeps its text as you wrote it, with no span per letter, so screen readers, search engines and find in page see the real words. While letters move a CSS filter hides the DOM text without removing it, and once they settle the element is real, selectable text again. Under reduced motion the effect is off.

Put the effect on the text itself: on a button with a background, wrap the label in a `span` and put `data-dio="magnet"` there, since the element's own box is hidden while the letters move.

The canvas cannot draw `background-clip: text`, `text-shadow`, `-webkit-text-stroke`, underlines or vertical writing modes the way the browser does. An element with any of the first four keeps its text as it is, and a warning in development names the reason; an underline is hidden while the letters move. Keep it to headings, links and short lines: over 1500 letters the text stays as it is. `style` rebuilds the effect when it changes.
