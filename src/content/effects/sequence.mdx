## Recipes

### Take a product apart on scroll

The usual page is a tall section with a sticky stage the height of the screen. `sequence` plays over the pin of that section, so 400vh gives the frames three screens of scroll. An `end` below 1 shows the last frame early and holds it while the reader takes in the heading.

```html
<section
  class="explode"
  data-dio-section
  style="height: 400vh"
>
  <div
    class="stage"
    style="position: sticky; top: 0; height: 100vh"
  >
    <img
      src="/seq/watch/01.avif"
      data-dio="sequence"
      data-dio-frames="/seq/watch/{01..72}.avif"
      data-dio-end="0.85"
    />
    <h2
      data-dio="text-reveal"
      data-dio-trigger="scroll"
    >Eleven parts, one hand.</h2>
  </div>
</section>
```

The `<img>` is the poster: it shows before the first frame decodes, without WebGL and with reduced motion. Make it the frame that tells the story on its own.

### Pack a short sequence into an atlas

Eighteen frames in two pages of 3 × 3 cost two requests instead of eighteen. A sparse set like this steps from frame to frame, and `blend` mixes neighbors by the fraction of the index, so it still moves smoothly. Here `progress` drives it, with no sticky stage: the watch opens while the frame crosses the middle of the viewport.

Atlas pages can be `.ktx2` too. Compressed pages take 4 to 8 times less GPU memory than `.avif` or `.webp`, which matters for long sequences on phones.

### Drive the frame from a tween

With `by` set to `manual` the scroll leaves it alone and `frame` picks the frame. Tween it with GSAP, tie it to a range input, or play it on a button.

```ts
const watch = app.effects.get(
  document.querySelector('.watch'),
  'sequence',
)
gsap.to(watch.params, {
  frame: 35,
  duration: 2.4,
  ease: 'power2.inOut',
})
```

## Notes

Each frame decodes off the main thread and only a window of `cache` frames stays decoded around the current one, two of them ahead in the scroll direction. A fast fling skips frames rather than stalling, and the nearest decoded frame shows meanwhile. On the `low` tier it loads every second frame, and under Save-Data or on 2G a quarter of them at up to 960 px.

Without `data-dio-section`, the section is the parent of the closest sticky ancestor. Add the attribute when the markup differs, for example when ScrollTrigger pins the stage, and on the section of any sticky stage whose copies should hold still while it is pinned. A pinned stage holds the canvas still during native scroll only when it fills half the viewport or more.

The sequence is decoration over a poster, so it needs no controls of its own. Put what the frames show into the `alt` of the `<img>` or into the text next to it: a screen reader reads that, never the frames.
